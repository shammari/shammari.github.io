The discrete logistic model

An interactive tool for MATH 316, weeks 2–3. Explore equilibria, stability, cycles and chaos in a difference equation with cobweb diagrams.

← MATH 316

A population that grows in discrete generations, and is held back as it nears its limit, can be modelled by the difference equation \(x_{n+1} = r\,x_n (1 - x_n)\). The same model can settle to an equilibrium, oscillate between several values, or never settle at all, depending only on r. This tool shows the cobweb diagram, the sequence itself and a summary of the long-run behaviour for every r. It supports learning outcomes 1 and 11.

The cobweb diagram follows the sequence: up to the curve, across to the diagonal, and repeat. The bottom panel shows, for each r, the values the sequence keeps visiting after it has run for a long time; click it to choose r. Drag the starting point along the horizontal axis of the cobweb diagram to change x0.

Things to try

  1. Set r = 2.5 and then r = 2.9. Both sequences approach the same kind of equilibrium, but in different ways. How do the cobweb diagrams differ? Relate this to the slope of the curve where it meets the diagonal.
  2. Find the equilibria by solving \(x = r x (1 - x)\). For which values of r is the nonzero equilibrium stable? Check your answer with the tool.
  3. Increase r slowly past 3. What does the sequence do now? Find where the 2-cycle gives way to a 4-cycle.
  4. Set r = 3.83. What do you see, and where is this in the bottom panel?
  5. Set r = 3.9 and turn on compare, which starts a second sequence at x0 + 0.001. How long do the two sequences stay close? What does this mean for long-term prediction?
  6. The continuous logistic equation \(dP/dt = rP(1 - P/K)\) never oscillates. Why can the discrete model oscillate when the continuous one cannot?

How it is computed

The sequence is computed directly from the difference equation. The equilibria are \(x^* = 0\) and \(x^* = 1 - 1/r\), and an equilibrium is stable when \(\lvert f'(x^*) \rvert < 1\), where \(f(x) = r x (1 - x)\). To find the long-run behaviour, the tool discards the first 1000 terms and then looks for a repeating cycle of length up to 64. The textbook form \(\Delta p_n = k\,p_n (M - p_n)\) becomes this model after the change of variable \(x_n = k p_n / (1 + kM)\), with \(r = 1 + kM\).

The tool runs entirely in your browser; nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. It is a learning aid and is not assessed.
