Tangent lines and the derivative

An interactive tool for MATH 101, weeks 4–6. Watch secant lines turn into the tangent line, compute the derivative as a limit of difference quotients, and see the graph of the derivative traced out from the slopes of f.

← MATH 101

The derivative of \(f\) at \(a\) is

\[f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a},\]

if this limit exists. It is the slope of the tangent line to \(y = f(x)\) at \((a, f(a))\), the limit of the slopes of secant lines, and the instantaneous rate of change of \(f\). The first part of this tool shows secant lines approaching the tangent line. The second part draws the graph of \(f'\) as the slope of \(f\) at each point. It supports learning outcomes 5 and 6.

From secant lines to the tangent line

Drag the point Q along the curve towards P, or use the slider for x. The table lists the slopes mPQ of the secant lines for Q closer and closer to P.

  1. For \(y = x^2\) at \(P(1, 1)\), compare the table with the textbook’s: as \(x \to 1\), the slopes approach 2. Turn on the tangent line and check its equation, \(y = 2x - 1\).
  2. For \(y = 3/x\) at \((3, 1)\), what do the slopes approach? Compare with the textbook’s tangent line, \(x + 3y - 6 = 0\).
  3. The position of a falling ball is \(s = 4.9t^2\) metres after \(t\) seconds. The slope of a secant line is an average velocity. What is the velocity at \(t = 5\)?
  4. For \(\lvert x\rvert\) at 0, compare the slopes from the left and from the right. Is there a tangent line? For \(\sqrt[3]{x}\) at 0, what happens to the slopes?

The derivative as a function

Move the point with the slider or by dragging it on the graph of f. The slope of the tangent line there is plotted as a point on the right. To practise, untick graph of f′, sketch it yourself while you move the point, then reveal it.

  1. For \(f(x) = x^3 - x\), where is \(f'(x) = 0\)? Where is \(f'\) negative? Match these with what the graph of \(f\) is doing. Turn on the textbook’s formula \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 1\) to check.
  2. Sketch the derivative of \(\sin x\) before revealing it. Which familiar function is it? What about the derivative of \(e^x\)?
  3. For \(\sqrt{x}\) and \(\lvert x\rvert\), where does \(f'\) fail to exist? Which of the three ways a function can fail to be differentiable (a corner, a discontinuity, a vertical tangent) is each?
  4. For \(\ln x\), compare the graph of \(f'\) with \(1/x\). For \(\sinh x\) and \(\cosh x\), which derivative is which?
  5. Type a function of your own and predict the shape of its derivative before revealing it.

How it is computed

The secant slope is \(m_{PQ} = [f(x) - f(a)]/(x - a)\). The derivative is approximated by one-sided difference quotients with \(h = 10^{-6}\): if the quotients from the left and from the right agree, their average is used as \(f'(a)\); if they differ, the tool reports a corner, and if they grow without bound, a vertical tangent. The graph of \(f'\) is drawn from the central difference \([f(x + h) - f(x - h)]/(2h)\) at several hundred points. The textbook’s formulas are included for comparison only; the tool does not differentiate symbolically.

The tool runs entirely in your browser; nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. It is a learning aid and is not assessed.
