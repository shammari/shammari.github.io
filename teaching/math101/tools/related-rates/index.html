Related rates

An interactive tool for MATH 101, week 7. Animate the textbook's related rates problems (the sliding ladder, the filling tank, the inflating balloon, two cars and a searchlight) and see each rate as the slope of a graph against time.

← MATH 101

In a related rates problem, two or more quantities change with time and are linked by an equation. Differentiating that equation with respect to time \(t\), using the Chain Rule, links their rates of change, so a rate we know gives a rate we want. This tool animates the textbook’s examples. The left panel shows the situation; the right panel graphs the quantity we want against time, and its tangent line, whose slope is the rate we are asked to find. It supports learning outcome 7.

Things to try

  1. In the ladder problem, press textbook’s moment: the bottom is 6 ft from the wall and slides away at 1 ft/s. Follow the steps in the readout to find how fast the top slides down. Then play the animation. What happens to the speed of the top as it nears the floor? Is that realistic?
  2. In the conical tank, water flows in at a constant rate. Does the water level rise at a constant rate? When is it fastest, and why?
  3. For the balloon, air goes in at 100 cm³/s. How fast is the radius growing when the radius is 25 cm? Why does the radius grow more and more slowly?
  4. For the two cars, find how fast the distance between them is shrinking when car A is 0.3 mi and car B is 0.4 mi from the intersection. Why is the answer not simply 50 + 60 = 110 mi/h?
  5. For the searchlight, when is the light turning fastest? Explain why with the picture.
  6. In each problem, compare the rate in the readout (from the differentiated equation) with the slope of the tangent line on the graph. Why must they agree? Change the parameters and check again.

How it is computed

Each problem follows the textbook’s strategy: name the quantities, write an equation that relates them, differentiate it with respect to \(t\), and substitute the values at the moment of interest. Time is measured from the start of the animation, and the quantity whose rate is given changes at that constant rate. For the tank and the balloon the volume grows at the given rate, and the height or radius is found from it. The rate in the readout comes from the differentiated equation; the graph is drawn from the positions themselves, so the slope of its tangent line, computed independently by a central difference, is a check on the calculus.

The tool runs entirely in your browser; nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. It is a learning aid and is not assessed.
